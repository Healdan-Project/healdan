Kestrel Software Ltd – Series A – the term sheet from Merrowdown Ventures LLP and Pellridge Capital Partners LP

Thank you for sending me the investors’ term sheet of 18 September 2026. You asked what I make of it. In short, it is a fair offer on usual terms, and I would accept it once you have raised the points below. Most of them are for the subscription agreement, which we will draft.

1.  Price. The pre-money valuation of £16,000,000 counts the 1,000,000 shares in the option pool as if they were issued, which gives the price of £1.60 a share. The pool therefore dilutes you, Tobias and the two angels, and not the investors. That is usual. Whether £16,000,000 is enough is for you and Tobias to judge, not a legal question, but I see nothing in how it is built to resist.

2.  Liquidation preference. A 1x non-participating preference is the usual kind. The investors get their money back first or convert into ordinary shares, whichever is better for them, but never both. Resist any move to a higher multiple or to a participating preference.

3.  Anti-dilution. Weighted-average protection is the fairer kind, because it adjusts the price only in proportion to how much cheaper the later shares are. Full ratchet would be far worse for you. I would accept this.

4.  Warranties. It is normal for founders to give warranties, but yours should be capped and limited in time. I suggest we propose that claims must be made within 18 months, that each of you is liable for no more than a year’s salary, and that no claim under £10,000 counts. Expect the investors’ solicitors to ask for more.

5.  Non-competes. Twelve months after you leave is acceptable, if it is limited to Kestrel’s own business. I would not agree to longer.

6.  The board. One investor director is to be expected. Pellridge, as the smaller investor, will probably ask for a seat as an observer, who can attend but cannot vote. I would agree to that if the observer is bound by confidentiality. Resist a second investor director, which would leave you and Tobias level with the investors.

7.  Consent matters, costs and exclusivity. The list of decisions needing the lead investor’s consent is reasonable for an investment of this size. I would ask that the yearly budget is left to the board. The cap of £25,000 on the investors’ legal costs and exclusivity to 2 November 2026 are both reasonable for a completion aimed at 23 October.

8.  Due diligence. The investors will look hard at two things. The first is who wrote your software. Anyone who is not an employee and has written part of Kestrel Rota must have signed an assignment of their rights to the company. Please check, and tell me about anyone who has not. The second is your standard customer licence terms, especially on liability and customer data. We are reviewing those separately, under matter 1003.2, and I will need a copy.

Please also send me a capitalisation table on the term sheet’s numbers, showing the options granted and those not yet allocated. If you are content, I suggest you sign the term sheet before the offer lapses on 25 September.
